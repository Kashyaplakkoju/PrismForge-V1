# Creating and importing 3D face filters

The current lab is a browser preview. Its camera input can be PrismForge Virtual Camera or another visible system camera. Importing a model here does not add it to the Windows app or publish the lab's filtered frames to a virtual camera; native integration is a separate step.

The five upstream filters are pinned to [Jeeliz commit 6f2695120b992511fd6cb1fd80c600bb957cd08c](https://github.com/jeeliz/jeelizFaceFilter/tree/6f2695120b992511fd6cb1fd80c600bb957cd08c). The full notices are in `site/NOTICES.txt` and `site/vendor/JEELIZ-LICENSE.txt`. They were not generated by an image AI.

Orbit and Prism were built in code: curved shells, thick plates, rings and other meshes receive standard Three.js materials and are attached to the tracked head. Their visible accessory geometry is original; they reuse the pinned Jeeliz head proxy for approximate depth occlusion. The original procedural source is editable rather than an independently exported GLB. No personal head scan or hair reconstruction is performed.

## The quickest way to make another one

1. Choose a design and use **Copy AI model prompt** in the lab. Replace the design placeholder with a precise description, such as an original brushed-metal fox mask with open eyes. The complete prompt lives in `experiments/jeeliz-3d-lab/site/model-authoring.js`.
2. Give that prompt to Antigravity, Gemini or another AI that can produce a Blender script or a downloadable 3D asset. A text answer or a rendered picture is not a 3D model. A coding AI can generate mesh vertices, materials and a Blender export script; it must run that script or a real binary GLB exporter to deliver the model. A reproducible Python/Node generator is also acceptable when it creates actual geometry, normals, UVs, materials and embedded textures. If its exporter cannot run, keep delivery pending until the real GLB exists.
3. Inspect the exported GLB in a fresh empty Blender scene. Check front, side and looking-down views, actual eye openings, material appearance and texture embedding. [Blender's GLB exporter](https://docs.blender.org/manual/en/5.1/addons/import_export/scene_gltf2.html) can place mesh data and image textures in a single binary file and exports the glTF Y-up convention. Blender itself uses different authoring axes; the prompt explains the conversion.
4. Stop the active lab preview, choose **Upload your own**, and select the `.glb` file. Use model motion first to inspect it without a camera, then use the live camera and fit controls. Adjust the model's size, position and rotation; keep its scale uniform so its volume stays intact.
5. Turn slowly left/right and look up/down. Check that it remains seated, the eye openings stay useful, and it hides on tracking loss. A model that works only in a front-facing screenshot has not passed fitting review.

The recommended authoring budget is 100,000 exported vertices and 100,000 triangles or fewer, with one or two 2048px texture atlases. Hard importer limits are 25 MiB per GLB, 150,000 drawn vertices and triangles including instances, 256 mesh primitives, 512 nodes, 16 images/textures, a 4096px maximum image side and 48 MiPixels (about 50 million pixels) across images. The imported scene is static. It cannot add expression animation merely because the original download included a rig.

Use a self-contained GLB 2.0 with triangle meshes, embedded PNG/JPEG textures and ordinary metallic/roughness materials. External resources, animation, skeletons, morph targets, sparse accessors and compressed mesh/texture formats are outside this importer. The AI prompt deliberately uses a conservative subset. A model can need a Blender conversion or simplification even when its filename ends in `.glb`.

The lab centres the complete model's bounding box and applies one uniform face-sized scale as a starting fit. The three supplied cap files are recognised by exact SHA256 and receive their original forehead-band anchors and facing correction; other files require manual placement. Reset restores that asset's starting fit. Different helmets, masks and ears therefore need individual placement. The current tracker provides one rigid head pose, not dense personalised landmarks, a complete head/hair surface or hand occlusion. Fitting controls cannot repair a wrong model shape, enormous eye holes or missing geometry.

## Can Sketchfab models be used?

Yes, a downloaded Sketchfab model can be imported when its GLB matches the supported static, self-contained format. If the download has external textures, an animation rig, unsupported compression or excessive geometry, import it into Blender, bake the desired static pose/materials, simplify it and export a compatible GLB. A full character needs its head or accessory isolated; uploading the whole character does not turn it into a face filter.

For models you want bundled with the saleable native app, supply the file **and** its model page, creator, exact licence and required attribution. Downloadable is not a blanket commercial-distribution permission. Sketchfab's [download guidelines](https://sketchfab.com/developers/download-api/guidelines) require Creative Commons attribution to follow models into AR filters. Its [licence agreement](https://sketchfab.com/licenses) places separate restrictions on editorial use and distributing extractable standalone assets; depicted brands and characters are not automatically cleared. Review the particular model's terms before selecting it for the bundle. The experimental Jeeliz collection is not a completed commercial-asset clearance list.

For original AI-generated models, retain the build script, source file, generation tool terms and any borrowed asset records. Ask for original designs and textures and record what was actually produced; a prompt alone does not establish third-party rights or a successful export.
